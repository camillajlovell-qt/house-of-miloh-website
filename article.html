An Insider's Guide To Denia Like A Local

Where to Stay & Eat like a local.

There is a distinct shift that happens when you make your way into Dénia. For me, this isn't just a destination, it's where I grew up, lived, and the place I find myself packing up to move back to yet again. You get the best of both worlds here, from the massive mountain landscape on one side to the endless sandy stretches and rocky coves on the other.

While a lot of the area rushed to modernise, Dénia has beautifully held onto its old Spanish charm. Not only is it recognised as a UNESCO City of Gastronomy (the food scene here is truly unreal!), it's also the place where the smell of salt and the sound of long dinner conversations instantly resets my nervous system.

Whether you're looking for the ultimate slow paced Mediterranean summer or a winter refuge to switch off and breathe, here is my personal playbook for the town I call home.

Where to Stay

La Posada del Mar: A historic building sitting right at the base of the old castle with beautiful views of the port. Expect beautifully restored stone arches, massive windows, and a deep focus on silence and natural light. It feels much more like staying at a very chic, private coastal home than a traditional hotel.

Nomada House by SH Hoteles (Maison Marineta): Tucked right into the old town, it's a total dream for design lovers who want to be steps from the local buzz but surrounded by interior beauty. Expect crisp interiors, a fantastic artisanal breakfast, and a very cool rooftop bar. Did I mention you can mix your own drink?

Hotel Nou Romà: One of the area's hidden gems, tucked away in the narrow, car free alleys of the historic Les Roques neighbourhood. It's an ancient manor house that seamlessly weaves historic brickwork with contemporary furniture. I love that the leafy inner courtyard feels worlds away from the busy outdoor crowds in summer.

Hotel Los Ángeles Dénia: A literal front row seat to the Mediterranean, located about 4 kilometres up the coast along the sandy stretches of Las Marinas. Expect a relaxed, light filled vibe with a touch of classic coastal elegance. Waking up to the actual sound of the waves is complete magic.

Eats

Tasca Els Tomassets: A buzzing tapas spot on the famous Calle Loreto. It is the ideal spot for traditional local plates and soaking up the lively evening atmosphere.

Tasca Les Monges: Proper Spanish home cooking in a cosy tavern setting. Order the fresh salads and warm tortilla paired with a cold glass of crisp Verdejo.

Restaurante Mena: Perched on the rocky cliffs at the very end of Les Rotes. It is an absolute institution for traditional rice dishes and local tapas enjoyed right over the crashing waves. Book a terrace table ahead of time for a lovely Mediterranean lunch.

Pont Sec: A masterclass in traditional, local landscape cuisine by chef Pep Romany. This is the spot to try authentic coques (the Valencian sister to pizza, topped with wild herbs and local salted fish).

El Baret de Miquel: An absolute must if you can manage to snag a table. Run by Michelin starred chef Miquel Ruiz, this casual tavern serves up incredibly creative, high end tapas without any pretension.

Els Magazinos: A lively, open air food market tucked into the old town. It's an alleyway filled with distinct food stalls, tiny bars, and terraces. It is my favourite stop for a casual drink before having dinner at A La Fresca.

Primera Línea: True to its name, this brilliant spot is nestled directly on the sandy beach of Las Marinas. It beautifully pairs a relaxed beach vibe with incredible high quality traditional cooking and excellent paellas.

Mala Vita: My favourite spot for breakfast or a coffee after a morning walk along the marina. Pop back at sunset for a cold drink right on the water.

Heladería Verdú: An absolute institution in Denia serving proper artisan ice-cream on Marqués de Campo since 1953. Grab a glass of Spain's famous ice cold horchata while you stroll under the trees.

Explore

Les Rotes: Skip the crowded sandy beaches for an evening and stroll along the rocky coastline of Les Rotes. Grab a cold drink at Helios on your way, sit right by the water, and watch the sun go down over the Mediterranean.

Walk up Dénia Castle: Walk up the winding streets of the historic fortress overlooking the town, with beautiful views of the town and coastline.

Shopping along Calle Campos: A beautiful pedestrian street in the centre of town, great for strolling, people watching, and popping into little local boutiques before grabbing an outdoor table for drinks.
